## Who is Naya Kai

Naya Kai is the professional pseudonym I work under. It brings together two ideas that shape how I approach consulting and delivery.

In Sanskrit, *Naya* can refer to method, judgement, direction and prudent conduct. For me, it represents careful reasoning: framing the problem properly, following the evidence and making decisions that can be explained.

*Kai* draws on the Japanese character 会, associated with meeting, gathering and association. It represents the shared work required to make change stick: people, processes and technology working together.

Together, Naya Kai reflects my belief that strong decisions need both *sound reasoning* and *systems that people can use*.

It is pronounced *NAY-ah KYE*.

## What I do

I help asset-intensive organisations turn data and AI investments into usable decision-support systems and measurable progress.

My work spans strategy through to delivery: framing the problem, sourcing and preparing data, designing the architecture, and building the pipelines, models and reporting that make it operational. I focus on solutions that are useful after the engagement ends, with clear ownership, auditable decisions and systems that client teams can operate and improve.

I use short delivery cycles and apply Scrum where it improves outcomes. Governance is visible from the outset, documentation is lean and useful and deployment and handover are designed for long-term client ownership.

## Approach to AI

AI and large language models are powerful tools, but they are not a substitute for sound data, clear accountability or good system design.

I approach AI delivery as an engineering and governance problem as much as a capability opportunity. Before scaling a use case, I consider whether the underlying data is suitable, how sensitive information is protected, how outputs are evaluated, who remains accountable for decisions, and what controls are needed to operate the solution safely.

My current work and learning include responsible AI, cybersecurity-aware delivery, data readiness for AI, retrieval-augmented generation and agentic workflows with defined scope, evaluation and human oversight.
